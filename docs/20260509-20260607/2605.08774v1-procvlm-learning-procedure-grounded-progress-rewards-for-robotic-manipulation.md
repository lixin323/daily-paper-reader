## 摘要
长时域机器人操作需要密集的反馈来反映任务在其程序化阶段中的进展，而不仅仅是最终结果是否成功。现有的奖励模型通常依赖于轨迹级别的成功标签或基于时间的插值，这可能会混淆经过时间与真实任务进展，因此无法捕获未完成的步骤、停滞和失败状态。我们提出了ProcVLM，一种进度感知的视觉-语言模型，它学习以程序化进展作为密集奖励信号用于操作。ProcVLM不是从最终结果或时间代理中推导出进度，而是将进度估计建立在程序化结构和阶段内视觉变化上，并进一步采用先推理后估计的范式，在估计任务进度之前推断剩余原子动作。具体来说，我们通过合成帧级子任务语义标注、根据子任务结构分配进度预算、以及基于子任务内视觉变化分配每个预算来构建这种监督。为了大规模训练ProcVLM，我们构建了一个标准化的程序化监督合成流水线，并从30个具身数据集中构建了ProcCorpus-60M，包含6000万标注帧，从中我们推导出ProcVQA用于程序感知预训练，以进度估计为核心任务，同时辅以动作分割和未来规划。在ProcVQA和奖励模型基准上的实验表明，ProcVLM改进了具身程序化推理，并产生了比代表性基线更具区分性的轨迹内部进度估计，支持其作为下游奖励引导策略优化的密集奖励模型。项目页面：https://procvlm.github.io/

## Abstract
Long-horizon robotic manipulation requires dense feedback that reflects how a task advances through its procedural stages, not merely whether the final outcome is successful. Existing reward models often rely on trajectory-level success labels or time-based interpolation, which can conflate elapsed time with true task progress and therefore fail to capture unfinished steps, stagnation, and failure states. We present ProcVLM, a progress-aware vision-language model that learns procedure-grounded progress as a dense reward signal for manipulation. Rather than deriving progress from terminal outcomes or temporal proxies, ProcVLM grounds progress estimation in procedural structure and intra-stage visual change, and further adopts a reasoning-before-estimation paradigm that infers the remaining atomic actions before estimating task progress. Specifically, we construct this supervision by synthesizing frame-level subtask-semantic annotations, assigning progress budgets according to subtask structure, and distributing each budget based on intra-subtask visual change. To train ProcVLM at scale, we build a standardized procedural supervision synthesis pipeline and construct ProcCorpus-60M from 30 embodied datasets with 60M annotated frames, from which we derive ProcVQA for procedure-aware pretraining, with progress estimation as the central task alongside action segmentation and future planning. Experiments on ProcVQA and reward-model benchmarks show that ProcVLM improves embodied procedural reasoning and yields more discriminative trajectory-internal progress estimates than representative baselines, supporting its use as a dense reward model for downstream reward-guided policy optimization.
